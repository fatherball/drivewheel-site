How to read
the Drive Wheel.

A whole football game on one circle. Where a drive sits is when it happened; how far it reaches is where it got to.

Chicago at San Francisco, Week 17, 2025.

Around

Time is the angle

Kickoff is at twelve. The game runs clockwise, a full turn for sixty minutes, so halftime lands at the bottom. A wide wedge is a long drive; a narrow one went three and out.

Outward

Distance is the field

The center is the offense's own goal line and the rim is the end zone they are attacking. Each dot is a snap, and the line traces the drive working its way out. The heavier ring halfway out is the fifty.

At the edge

The rim is the outcome

Every drive ends on a band at the rim, thick and colored if it scored and thin grey if it didn't, labeled with the result and the new score. The ring just inside shows who was ahead at that moment.

Below the wheel

Score, and overtime

Under each wheel is the running score through regulation, lined up so its halftime matches the wheel's. If the game went to overtime, a straight field follows: yard lines across, clock running down, the two teams attacking opposite ends the way a traditional drive chart shows it.

The marks

What each shape means

  • A first down. Solid dots moved the chains.
  • Any other snap. Hollow dots did not.
  • A penalty. The triangle points at the team the flag was on — including a kickoff put out of bounds, which hands the ball over near midfield.
  • A turnover, at the spot it happened.
  • A return, dashed, in the returning team's colour — kickoffs, punts and turnovers alike.
  • A field goal attempt, at the spot of the kick.
  • A scoring drive, in the scoring team's color.
  • A drive that didn't score.
  • The try after a touchdown — XP or 2PT, grey if it missed.
  • A kickoff won by the kicking team — a fumbled return or a recovered onside kick. This is what explains one team getting two drives in a row.
  • The vertical line splits the halves: kickoff at the top, halftime at the bottom.

Hovering a wedge gives you the drive; clicking any mark gives you the play it came from. On a touch screen, tap instead.
